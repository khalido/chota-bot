# Drawing 2 (v2): the wall plate (module `wall.mjs`, id prefix `w-`)

Replaces the easel. KO's idea: a wooden plate on the wall, everything on its face, slim low-profile speakers that look good and actually sound decent. Read DIR/RULES.md and DIR/common.mjs fully first (common.mjs now also has `dim()` for dimension lines; figure.css has `.dim`, `.dim-text`, `.mesh`, `.cavity`, `.acrylic`). Look at DIR/build-easel.mjs for the helper style (rod, path3, standoff, screwTop, circTop, pinRow) and reuse what fits by copying into your module.

## The contract (different from v1: it renders at runtime in the browser)

- Write `DIR/wall.mjs`: an ES module that imports only from `./common.mjs` (no fs, no Node APIs), and exports `renderWall(state)` returning `{ svg, callouts }`. `svg` is the full `<svg …>` string (viewBox `0 0 1300 950`), `callouts` is `[{n, name, fact}]` for the parts drawn in that state.
- `state = { cover: boolean, printerLid: number }`. `cover`: a clear acrylic cover (3 mm, `acrylic` class, hairline edges) over the boards row, on four brass standoffs; when false, the boards are bare. `printerLid`: 0 closed … 1 fully open (about 95°), showing the 80 mm paper roll inside.
- The page calls renderWall on every animation frame while a lid moves, so keep it pure and fast (string building only).
- Also write `DIR/build-wall.mjs` (Node) that imports renderWall and writes `wall.svg` for `{cover:true, printerLid:0}` and `wall-open.svg` for `{cover:false, printerLid:1}`, so `DIR/shot.sh wall 1300`, `shot.sh wall-open 1300` and `shot.sh wall 420` work. Look at every screenshot critically (dark and light) and fix what is wrong.

## Geometry (mm). u = from the plate's left edge, w = out from the wall (wall at w = 0), z = up from the plate's bottom edge

Use `frame(K, W, Dp)` with W = 640 and Dp large enough (say 220). Pick the kernel scale so the whole figure, with labels in the left and right margins, fills the viewBox.

- **Wall**: a light `plain` face behind everything (a PLANE_AT_W rect at w = 0, a bit larger than the plate), so the plate reads as hung. Optional faint hairline skirting; no clutter.
- **Plate**: 640 W × 360 H × 18 thick, oak-faced birch ply, at w = 30–48 (it stands 30 mm off the wall on a hidden French cleat; show a sliver of the cleat's shadow gap at the left edge and top). Draw its top edge and left edge as wood strips with a couple of `hair` ply laminations. Four brass screw heads near the corners.
- **Screen**: Waveshare 10.1" DSI (C) module, 235 × 161 (from KO's notes, unverified), on four brass standoffs 12 mm off the plate face, so its glass is at w ≈ 48 + 12 + 6. Bare PCB edge showing 2 mm on the left and bottom, no bezel. Lit `screen-on` with a literary-clock face ("06:52" big, two short `screen-text` lines), `halo` behind. Place it centred on u 110–345, z 165–326.
- **Speakers, slim**: two 3" full-range drivers mounted _through_ the plate into shallow sealed back-boxes hidden behind it (the 30 mm gap plus a 40 mm deep box; a `ghost` dashed outline of each back-box behind the plate is fine). On the face: a round brass-mesh grille Ø 90 flush in a turned brass ring, either side of the screen, centres at u ≈ 60 and u ≈ 395, z ≈ 245. Draw the mesh with the `.mesh` class plus a fine hole pattern (repetition, not noise). They read as two brass portholes, low profile (ring stands 3 mm proud).
- **Boards row** under the screen (z 40–140), each on brass M2.5 standoffs, parts protruding toward the viewer (+w):
  - Raspberry Pi 5 4GB (85 × 56 PCB, long side along u) at u 120–205, z 60–116, with the Active Cooler (fins, round fan), 40-pin header, USB/Ethernet stacks on its right short edge.
  - ReSpeaker XVF3800 mic board: round Ø ~ 70 (unverified) `pcb`, 4 mic holes at 90°, a ring of 12 small LEDs with 2–3 lit, at u ≈ 255, z ≈ 90. It is also the USB sound card (line out to the amp).
  - Amp: a small 2 × 5 W class-D board (≈ 40 × 30) at u ≈ 310–350, z 70–100. **Do not label it PAM8403.** Label "Amp" · "2×5 W, fed by the mic board".
  - FFC ribbon (`ffc`) from the screen's bottom edge down to the Pi's display connector, with a tiny 22→15-pin adapter board on the way. Short USB leads (`cable`) Pi ↔ mic board, mic board → amp, amp → each speaker (speaker wires disappear into the plate through small holes).
  - The acrylic cover (when `state.cover`): one clear box u 105–365, z 30–150, 20 mm proud of the tallest part, `acrylic` fill, brass standoff caps at its four corners. Draw it after the boards so they show through.
- **Controls**: on the plate face at the right of the boards row: WHO and WHAT wooden knobs (Ø 30, tick marks around each) at u ≈ 395 and u ≈ 440, z ≈ 95, and the 30 mm red PRINT arcade button (`accent`, brass collar Ø 40) at u ≈ 420, z ≈ 45. Pico 2 WH (51 × 21) on standoffs just above them at u 395–446, z 125–146.
- **Printer**: the MUNBYN ITPP098P mounted on the plate by its two base hanging holes (KO's notes say it supports wall mounting), so its base sits against the plate and its top faces the viewer: footprint on the plate u 470–610 (its 140 mm width) × z 150–330 (its 180 mm depth, front edge down), protruding 125 mm (its height) toward +w. Sizes are from a Newegg listing (180 L × 140 W × 125 H), not measured: keep them as constants at the top of the module so KO's measurement is a one-line change. Look of a generic 80 mm receipt printer: `dark` body, a top cover (the face toward the viewer) with a seam around it and a hinge at the far end (top, z ≈ 330), the paper exit with a serrated tear bar along its front edge (bottom, z ≈ 150), a small panel with 2 LEDs (one `led on`) and a FEED button beside the cover, a round cover-release button on one side. Paper (`paper`, with a few `paper-ink` lines) comes out of the exit and hangs down ~90 mm. When `printerLid > 0` the cover swings open about its hinge (rotating up and toward the viewer, 0…95°) and shows the cavity (`cavity`) with an 80 mm wide, Ø 80 paper roll (axis along u) and its paper path to the exit; while open, the hanging paper is still shown. Draw the roll as a cylinder: sample both end circles in 3D, project with Pt, fill the convex hull with `paper`, then draw the visible end as a circle in a PLANE_AT_U group with the 12 mm core.
- **Power**: one cable (`cable`) leaves the plate's bottom edge near u ≈ 600 (to a wall socket, out of frame), plus the official Pi PSU lead disappearing through a hole in the plate near the Pi. All other wiring is behind the plate.
- **Dimensions** with `dim()`: the plate's width (640) along its bottom edge, its height (360) along its left edge, its depth off the wall (48 plate, 173 to the printer's face) along the top. Labels short: "640", "360", "48 / 173".
