# Chota box: cut list for review

A counter-top wooden box for a Raspberry Pi 5 kitchen gadget. Outside: 424 W × 262 D × 184 H mm closed. Butt-jointed, glued and pinned/screwed, built by a hobbyist with a drill, a saw, a router (rebates) and clamps.

Layout: the left zone (244 mm wide) holds a recessed control deck (two dials, a needle meter, a 30 mm arcade button, toggles, LEDs) under a lid whose inner face is a 10.1" screen module (235 × 161, routed rebate, 4.5 mm lip). The lid is a 20 mm frame with a 6 mm ply skin, hinged on a fixed 42 mm hardwood strip along the back; it opens 110° and a brass chain stops it. Right zone: a printer bay (thermal receipt printer, listed 140 W × 180 D × 125 H, not measured yet; 40 mm behind it for plugs) under a service lid of the same construction, with the paper slot (90 × 5) in the service lid. Under the deck, front: one sealed speaker chamber (~1.9 L; a 6 mm ply back and top, the base, front wall, left end and divider as its other sides) for two front-mounted 3.5" drivers; the deck rests on 4 mm strips on its top. Behind it the Pi 5, a USB sound card, a small amp and a Pico. The mic board, a TALK button and a camera sit behind the front rim above the deck. No mains inside: the Pi's USB-C lead and one 24 V lead (printer + amp) come in through grommets in the back wall.
