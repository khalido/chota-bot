# Chota box: cut list for review

A counter-top wooden box for a Raspberry Pi 5 kitchen gadget. Outside: 356 W × 239 D × 165 H mm closed, on a 12 mm plinth set 10 mm in. Butt-jointed, glued and pinned/screwed, built by a hobbyist with a drill, a saw, a router (rebates) and clamps.

Layout: the left zone (200 mm wide) holds a recessed control deck (two dials, a needle meter, a 30 mm arcade button, toggles, LEDs, three small OLEDs) under a lid whose inner face is an 8" screen module (194 × 119, glass flush in a routed rebate, 3 mm of wood round it). The lid is a 20 mm frame with a 6 mm ply skin, hinged on a fixed 30 mm hardwood strip along the back; it opens 110° and a brass chain stops it. Right zone: the thermal printer (measured 140 W × 175 D × 130 H) located by blocks on the base, 25 mm behind it for plugs and 15 mm in front for what sits behind the bay front: one smoked-acrylic window with an LED-matrix face, a radar board and a camera behind it, and a knob, TALK and three buttons under it, under a service lid of the same construction with the paper slot (90 × 5) and a 4.2" e-ink. There is no divider: a hardwood post and rail carry the deck's right edge and the lids rest on the front wall and hinge strip. Under the deck, front: one sealed speaker chamber (~0.9 L; 6 mm ply back and top, a 12 mm end panel, with the base, front wall and left end as its other sides) for two 2.5" drivers mounted from inside the front wall. Behind it the Pi 5, a USB sound card, a small amp and a Pico. The mic board sits behind the front rim above the deck, with two pinholes. A 3 mm groove down the front wall under the lid seam splits the front into two panels. No mains inside: the Pi's USB-C lead and one 24 V lead (printer + amp) come in through grommets in the back wall.
