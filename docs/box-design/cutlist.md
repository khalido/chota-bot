# Chota box: cut list for review

A counter-top wooden box for a Raspberry Pi 5 kitchen gadget. Outside: 361 W × 254 D × 165 H mm closed. Butt-jointed, glued and pinned/screwed, built by a hobbyist with a drill, a saw, a router (rebates) and clamps.

Layout: the left zone (203 mm wide) holds a recessed control deck (two dials, a needle meter, a 30 mm arcade button, toggles, LEDs, three small OLEDs) under a lid whose inner face is an 8" screen module (194 × 119, routed rebate, 4.5 mm lip). The lid is a 20 mm frame with a 6 mm ply skin, hinged on a fixed 42 mm hardwood strip along the back; it opens 110° and a brass chain stops it. Right zone: the thermal printer (measured 140 W × 175 D × 130 H) located by blocks on the base, 40 mm behind it for plugs and 15 mm in front for a knob, three buttons and a radar board behind the bay front, under a service lid of the same construction with the paper slot (90 × 5) and a 4.2" e-ink. There is no divider: a hardwood post and rail carry the deck's right edge and the lids rest on the front wall and hinge strip. Under the deck, front: one sealed speaker chamber (~0.9 L; 6 mm ply back and top, a 12 mm end panel, with the base, front wall and left end as its other sides) for one front-mounted 2.5" driver, with an LED-matrix face beside it behind a glued acrylic window. Behind it the Pi 5, a USB sound card, a small amp and a Pico. The mic board, a TALK button and a camera sit behind the front rim above the deck. No mains inside: the Pi's USB-C lead and one 24 V lead (printer + amp) come in through grommets in the back wall.

Questions for the reviewer: Is the piece count and sizing right for these joints (do any pieces overlap or come up short)? Is 12 mm ply strong and stiff enough; would 9 mm do anywhere? Do the lids sit well with no divider under their meeting edges? Do the hinges and chain make sense at this weight?
